Evidence basis

Both tools are grounded in published rehabilitation research. Neither claims to be a clinically validated tool — they are wellness support tools designed to complement professional care.

Visual field trainer

Stroke Sight

Stroke Sight's hemianopia exercises are based on the compensatory saccadic scanning approach described in NICE NG236 (Stroke rehabilitation in adults, October 2023) and four decades of research:

  • Pambakian et al. (2004) — saccadic training, JNNP
  • Zihl et al. (2021) — 5-year follow-up on compensatory scanning training
  • Rowe et al. (2025) — SEARCH trial: both groups improved on structured scanning practice with clinical support

The neglect exercises use smooth pursuit eye movement training — an approach described in German Neurological Society guidance and in the Klinke et al. (2015) systematic review. Spatial auditory cues draw on the multisensory stimulation research of Bolognini and Tinelli.

Scotoma exercises provide awareness training around the blind spot area.

Clinical guideline context NICE NG236 (Stroke rehabilitation in adults, October 2023) describes eye movement therapy for stroke-related visual field loss. Stroke Sight's exercises are based on the compensatory scanning approach described in that literature; the app itself has not been clinically evaluated and is not endorsed by NICE.
Handwriting rehab

ReWrite

ReWrite provides structured tracing practice using principles from the graphomotor rehabilitation literature. Repetitive, task-specific practice is the strongest evidence base in stroke motor rehabilitation:

  • Langhorne, Bernhardt & Kwakkel (2011) — "Stroke rehabilitation," The Lancet, 377(9778). Establishes repetitive task-specific training as the primary evidence base for stroke motor recovery
  • Veerbeek et al. (2014) — systematic review supporting repetitive practice for upper limb motor recovery, PLoS ONE
  • Yancosek & Howell (2009) — fine motor assessment and rehabilitation, Journal of Hand Therapy

Session length is designed around typical post-stroke fatigue windows. Content is language-specific — not translated — supporting rehabilitation in the patient's first language.

First language matters. ReWrite is the first dedicated adult post-stroke handwriting rehabilitation app available in Welsh, Irish and Canadian French, alongside 16 other languages with real language-specific content.
National Aphasia Association. ReWrite is listed as a resource by the US National Aphasia Association. aphasia.org
For speech and language therapists

Writing practice as a language modality

ReWrite's Word Builder offers word-finding and spelling-by-hand practice: a word stem is completed by choosing the right ending, traced, then written from memory. For people with aphasia whose goals include written language, that practice can sit alongside your sessions — keeping writing active as an expressive modality between appointments, in the person's own language rather than a translation.

ReWrite supports independent practice. It does not assess or treat aphasia, and it is not a substitute for speech and language therapy — you set the goals; it helps with the repetitions.

Other resources

Independent free tools you may also want to mention

Eye-Search and Read-Right are free, university-developed web tools: Eye-Search offers visual-search practice for people with visual field loss, and Read-Right offers reading practice for hemianopic alexia. Both were developed entirely independently of Ansteyapps — we are not affiliated with them, and they have no connection to our apps. We mention them because patients are best served by knowing every free resource that exists.

eyesearch.ucl.ac.uk  ·  readright.ucl.ac.uk
