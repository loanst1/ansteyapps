Reading problems after stroke: strategies for hemianopic reading difficulty

When a visual field loss gets in the way of reading, a few simple techniques can help you keep your place and read more smoothly.

Why reading gets hard after a stroke

Reading depends on quick, accurate eye movements along and between lines. When a stroke causes visual field loss (hemianopia), those movements are disrupted, and reading becomes slow and effortful — a difficulty sometimes called hemianopic alexia. The vision problem, not the ability to understand words, is what gets in the way.

Right- and left-field loss affect reading differently

With right field loss, it’s hard to see the next words coming, so reading is slow and you lose flow along the line. With left field loss, the trouble is finding the start of the next line, so you skip lines or lose your place. Knowing which you have points to the strategy that helps most.

Strategies that help

Anchor the line

Run a finger or a ruler under the line as you read, and — for left field loss — mark the start of each line with a bright vertical line or your finger, returning to it before moving down.

Move the text, not just the eyes

Some people read more easily with the page tilted, or by scrolling text so words pass a fixed point. Reading on a phone or tablet lets you enlarge the text and adjust spacing.

Bigger, better-spaced text

Larger print with generous line spacing reduces the crowding that makes losing your place easier. Increase font size on digital devices without guilt — it’s a tool, not a defeat.

Reading practice tires the eyes. Short, frequent sessions with rest breaks build stamina more comfortably than long ones.

When to get help

An orthoptist or neuro-optometrist can confirm the type of field loss and recommend reading strategies or optical aids. Ask your GP or stroke team for a referral, and get urgent advice for any sudden new vision change.

Common questions

Why can I read individual words but not sentences after my stroke?
Field loss disrupts the eye movements that carry you along and between lines, so flow breaks down even though word recognition is intact.
Do reading strategies restore my vision?
No — they help you read around the field loss using anchoring, larger text and scanning. They don’t restore the lost field.
Can an app help with reading after stroke?
Structured scanning practice, like Stroke Sight’s reading and search exercises, can help build the eye-movement habits that reading relies on.
